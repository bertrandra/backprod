The Demonstration World

Five products — one of them deployed beside the platform — three organisations, one person per role, four live subscriptions with the invoices they raised, five projects — the world composer run demo:seed builds and the installer seeds. This page is who is in it, how to sign in as each of them, and what each one sees.

Seeding it

From a shell, against a migrated database (DATABASE_DSN set, or in .env):

composer run demo:seed          # seed it into an empty database
composer run demo:reset         # wipe the business tables first, then seed

Or from the console: signed in as the platform administrator, Console → Demo → Reset the demonstration world does the same as demo:reset — after a second, explicit yes, because it empties every business table, the reader's own account included, and signs everybody out. It then lists who to sign in as. It is refused while the platform hosts a product that is not one of the five: that is somebody's real product.

The seeder refuses a database where any of the five product codes already exists, so it never doubles a catalogue. It reads the world back afterwards — subscriptions active, invoices numbered, every offer advertised, every product with its tax position and a project schema version, every offer granting the projects quota the workspace reads, every project where it was made, every role held by somebody — and fails loudly if anything does not hold. Reference data (permissions, roles, EU VAT rates) comes from migrations and is never touched.

The SiteGround installer (setup.php) seeds the same world after writing .env and running the migrations. It asks for no product and no account of your own: you sign in as the people below.

The five products

Each has the same three-plan catalogue — Starter, Pro, Scale — with four features (max_projects, exports and users as quotas — Starter covers its buyer alone, Pro three people, Scale everybody — white_label as a switch), three published and advertised offers, its own supplier identity for invoices, a French VAT position (OSS-registered, digital services, EUR) and one accepted project schema version. The quota is spelled max_projects because that is what the workspace asks before it stores a project: until 2026-09-22 the demo priced projects, which nothing reads, and no subscriber could create one. Prices differ so the five are distinguishable on the storefront and in the console.

Plan is deployed beside the platform (ADR-051): it has an application address, https://plan.raillard.org, which the card above the project list offers as a door — the switcher stays on the platform, where Plan's subscription, invoices, members and projects live — and its catalogue also meters plan.documents — the quota Plan's own server reports usage against with its product key (20 on Starter, 200 on Pro, unlimited on Scale). Its key and its webhook are issued from Console → Products → Plan, not seeded: both are shown once and belong in Plan's environment.

The three organisations

A tenant holds the products the platform assigned it, and a member of the tenant is mirrored onto every one of them (ADR-047). All three have a billing profile and a B2B French tax profile, so any can be invoiced. Each lives at its own URL root (ADR-049): Acme is the default tenant, so it answers at /; Globex answers at /globex/, Initech at /initech/. All admit newcomers at once (join policy OPEN, the default): a sign-up at either root is a member — a USER, who may buy — there and then. Switch a policy on the Organisation screen to see the other three.

The nine people

Each organisation has its administrator and at least one member — Acme and Globex two, Initech one — and the platform has its one administrator. Nobody holds two authorities at once: a platform role never grants a tenant membership, and the reverse (non-negotiable #22). tenant roles sign in to an organisation's screens; platform roles land in the console. The addresses are the operator's own (2026-09-19), so a reset link or an invitation the platform sends lands somewhere real.

Everybody with a membership opens on Plan. Their profile's default product names it, so signing in at an address that mentions no product lands on the one deployed beside the platform — the only one whose screens are elsewhere, and so the only one whose card, link and subscription have anything to show. Change it on your own profile and the next sign-in obeys. The platform administrator has none: a default product is a choice among memberships, and staff hold none.

On any deployment that is reachable by someone else, change the platform administrator's password before anything else — the installer's success page offers the form — or, since 2026-09-19, use Forgot your password? on the sign-in page, which sends a link to the account's address once the mail channel is configured (MAIL_DSN).

Things worth looking at

  1. The storefront is one organisation's. Signed out, / is Acme's window — a dropdown of the five products it holds and the advertised offers of the chosen one — /globex/ is Globex's, with three, and /initech/ Initech's, with Plan alone and no dropdown. Only products with something sellable are listed.
  2. A stranger signs up and pays. At /globex/, choose an offer and create an account: the pay step opens on the same page, on the session the sign-up issued — a USER of Globex on both its products, with billing.pay. As Ada, switch Globex to Ask an administrator on the Organisation screen: the next sign-up lands on "waiting for Globex" and appears under Members as a request to accept or decline.
  3. The platform can give what it does not sell. As the platform admin, open Acme in Console → Tenants and grant Ceres's Pro features until next month; as the ACME tenant admin in Ceres, the Subscription screen says provided by the platform and the workspace opens without a purchase.
  4. The invoice says where the money stands. Download 2026-000001 as the ACME tenant admin: the PDF carries a band — en attente de paiement with its due date — and after the payment below, payée le … with the reference. The stored document underneath never changes.
  5. The console follows the switcher. As the platform admin, the top-left switcher lists all five products, and Setup, Catalogue, Storefront and Invoicing follow it. Retire one from Console → Products and watch it marked so.
  6. A product beside the platform. As any member of Acme, choose Plan in the switcher: the shell stays here and shows what the platform keeps for Plan — its projects, its subscription, its members. The card above the list is the door: Open Plan leaves for plan.raillard.org with ?product=plan and nothing else, and the cookie signs you in there — no token travels. Open Terrasse sud — parcelle AE 101 and Open in Plan carries ?project= as well, so Plan opens that terrace and not the last one it saw.
  7. A tenant with several products. As the ACME tenant admin the switcher offers five products; as the Globex tenant admin, three; as Initech's, one. Assign Ceres to Globex from Console → Tenants (as Sam) and Ada's switcher gains it — her membership was mirrored onto it.
  8. Withdrawing a product is refused while service is owed. Unassign Atlas from Acme and the console answers PRODUCT_IN_USE: the Pro subscription is still running.
  9. Money moves only on the provider's word. With Stripe configured (sandbox keys, stripe listen), take a payment on 2026-000001 as Ada with card 4242 4242 4242 4242; the invoice turns paid when the webhook lands, not when the form closes. 4000 0000 0000 9995 is declined and offers Try again.
  10. Staff reads leave a trail. As the platform admin, open a tenant with a purpose and reference, then read the access log.
  11. A subscription covers people. As ACME user1, buy the Pro seat — three people — and add user2 from the picker, then somebody by address: they get an account and a link to choose a password.

The demonstration page

/demo shows all of the above to anybody, with no sign-in — every product with its offers on sale, every organisation with a link to its root, its products, subscriptions and people with their roles. It is off on every deployment until a platform administrator switches it on from Console → Demo (behind staff.demo.publish), because everywhere else that is a member's answer; while it is off, /demo is a 404. Switch it on here, and hand a visitor the one link.

Where it is defined

The one definition of the world — products, organisations, people, subscriptions, projects. This page describes it; when one changes, so does the other.

Builds it and reads it back: the rows through PostgresDemoFixtures, the subscriptions and invoices through the services that own them, then the checks.
